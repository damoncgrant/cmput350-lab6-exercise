Answer written questions here:
    1. In our doubling-iteration scheme, why do we start from a low number? 
    Consider effects on rendering time/responsiveness and that we must 
    finish a call to mandelbrotSmooth for each pixel before going on to 
    the next frame.
We start low to compute each pixel very quickly at the start, too many iterations and the
frames will render slowly and be slow to respond to user input. 

    2. Propose a way to increase efficiency of rendering for high iteration counts, 
    exploiting that the rendering of each pixel is independent of the other pixels, 
    and assuming you can use multiple computers/cores/threads at the same time 
    (whatever level of abstraction you're comfortable with). In particular, suppose 
    you can coordinate these computers by having one of them delegate tasks to others
    and wait for their results.
If we are able to compute each pixel on its own, we could divide the image into chunks and
have a different worker for each chunk rather than having to iterate through every pixel per frame.
In theory if we had 4 computers, one per chunk, we could render each frame 4x as fast although
it wouldn't be exactly 4x since there is work that needs to happen to distribute the tasks and
output the results into one place.

    3. At “farther away” zooms with 1 centered sample per pixel, some regions of 
    the set will look like speckled noise (try zooming into the zoomed view!), 
    with jaggies at the boundary of the set. Consider and discuss what would happen 
    if we sampled at the center of 4 subpixels per pixel and averaged the colors 
    instead, from both a visual perspective and a performance perspective.
This will make the imaging look smoother since we are getting more accurate colors by 
doing more samples. This will negatively harm performance though since we are doing 4
calculations per pixel instead of just 1.
